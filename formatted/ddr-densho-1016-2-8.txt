AD: I love some of the stories that you told about your involvement here at the church. Tell us how you got involved at Highland Park and what kind of community that provided for you.
SE: Well, at the time when I first came here, the people here, the Japanese here in Spokane weren't too happy to see us come because they felt that it would jeopardize their chance of not going to camp if too much, too many, influx of Japanese came over to Spokane. So they were afraid for their own selves, so we weren't very welcome here. And when I worked at the Blairs' they helped me get my sisters here, and then they also said that I could invite my folks here. So that's how my family all got to come to Spokane is because of this Mr. Blair, Johnny Blair, who was an attorney. And I had only been working for them for two weeks, I think. In fact, Mrs. Blair wasn't even back home from seeing her son off. But he invited them to come, and said they could stay at their summer home out at Liberty Lake. So I called my father and told him that if they would like, they can come. And so they came to Spokane and were not, didn't have to go to camp.
AD: Tell us about the youth group here at Highland Park Church, and what did you do here? Maybe give the name so people know what you're talking about.
[Interruption]
SE: At first, though, I don't know, we didn't feel comfortable here because we were sort of outsiders. But we came to the church on Sundays and it was quite a while before I became really active in the church here. It was after I married that I came back to church real actively, because after we got married, and then, and lived out where we do, it was difficult because we were farming and church, we were very busy on Sundays because of the orders to take into the wholesale houses. So I took my children to Sunday school over in our neighborhood. So while they were growing up, I don't think they knew ever Japanese are any different from the children in the neighborhood because they didn't get to associate with the Japanese community 'til they were quite a bit older.
AD: What did you do with... what were some of the things that you did with the youth group?
SE: Youth group here? I really didn't do too much. [Laughs] I wished I did. But no, I returned to this church in 1990. So through those years, when the kids were growing up, I supported the church in our Iron Springs neighborhood. And so my girls never got to come to church here.
AD: What about when you were just moved to Spokane, during the war years? How was the church important for you during the war?
SE: During the war? I have to tell the truth, because I didn't feel comfortable. We didn't come here that much. We came to the special events and things, but the church really... at the time that we came to Spokane, the Reverend John Cobb and his wife were very instrumental, young people adjust to things here. And when my husband and I planned to get married, Mrs. Cobb planned the groom's supper because none of our parents were here. And so she planned this beautiful dinner for us. They were like mother and father to the young people that came here seeking help. So those students that were going to WSU all came up here on the weekends, and then that same lady, that Mrs. Miyake, she opened her home to the young people that came up. So many of the girls here married students from WSU.
