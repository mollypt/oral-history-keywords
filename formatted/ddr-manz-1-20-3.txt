AL: Yeah,   I'd like to, as we go on, loop back maybe and talk a little bit   more about some of the differences in your experience and her   experience, based on your age and your experience before the war.   It sounds like, by the time the war came, if you had those kind of   possessions, that your father must have been doing well in his   business?
TO: He was   doing well and the family was very comfortable. And, but, you know,   what little monies that they were able to keep was all exhausted   when we were released. My, my father and mother were here on a   trader's or business visa, so the catch-22 for them is no business,   no entitlement to a business visa, ergo, you need to be deported.   Meanwhile, they have four children, American-born children. So we   were taken to live with one of his younger uncles who remained in   the U.S.
AL: And   this is after Pearl Harbor?
TO: Yes,   no, he, after he came out to work on the railroad, ended up in a   little train stop in the northeast corner of Nevada. Elko, Nevada.   And so he was there and he was beyond the area where you needed to   be evacuated and interned, and so when my parents had to go to San   Francisco to fight deportation, we went to live with my   uncle...
AL: Okay,   this, but this is after, after the war started?
TO: After   we were released, after '45.
AL: Okay,   yeah.
