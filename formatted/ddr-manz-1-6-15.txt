JA: What do you think you learned from your experience at Manzanar?
AM: Well, I think in spite of all these conditions and things like that, not only myself but I'm sure a lot of other people felt the same way, we tried to make the best of the situation. So, what I did was I got, I got together with a friend who collected records, and I happened to have an amplifier. So we formed a partnership and became DJ. [Laughs] So we would get hired to play at dances and things like that, and I really enjoyed that. So even to this day he sends me e-mail talking about old times and things like that. But, you know, I think what I got over was the fact that I was very backward with people and I -- maybe the camp life living all together, close together like that maybe made me open up myself with other people and, you know, you can't go living your own life by yourself, you have to have friends. And so the friends that I made in camp are very close friends of mine. Yeah.
JA: What kind of records did you play?
AM: Oh, they were mostly like Glenn Miller, Artie Shaw, all those popular songs in those days, yeah.
JA: Big band stuff?
AM: Big band, yeah. So, well, we didn't charge very much for doing the DJ, you know, just enough to buy, keep the record supply up. [Laughs]
JA: That's good, that's good.
