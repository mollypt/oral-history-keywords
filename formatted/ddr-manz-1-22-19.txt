AL: Did you ever get to leave camp when you were living there?
YK: Yes. We did that for the newspaper. The paper was a weekly, came out on Saturday. All week we collected and edited, planned for the paper, and Friday nights we would go out to Cody, which had a print shop. And by the way, there was a Japanese boy working at the print shop, because he was a printer in San Jose, so he found a nice job in Cody. So because he was there, it was easy to contact him to get the job done. So we would take our copies and naturally, our shopping list. [Laughs] So we'd go, go the print shop, oh, about three or four o'clock and give them the copy, which is edited and all scrutinized by Bill Hosokawa, he was our editor-in-chief. Vaughn Mechau was our Caucasian supervisor.
AL: What was his name?
YK: Vaughn Mechau, M-E-C-H-A-U, first name, Mechau, you pronounce it. He was a jokester. First time we went there, he says, "Jack, you know what print lice are?" I've been in a printer shop, said, "Lice?" [Laughs] You have old slides and water in between, and he said, "Look at the lice," in the slides, and he would squeeze the slides together and water would shoot up and get us in the face. [Laughs] I laughed and he would laugh. Jokester. So, after we made sure that the guys were, the linotype was what the copies were to be, we would go out to eat. He would take us to one of the best, better places in Cody. Well, after we ate our dessert, apple pie a la mode, oh my god.
AL: Who paid for that? That must have been expensive.
YK: Well, I don't think it was that expensive.
AL: It wasn't?
YK: Yeah. Well, you know, way up there. But I think the newspaper did, somehow. So we would get treated to a movie theater, movie theater, all wild west movies. [Laughs] Bill Hart, Tom Mix, who else?
AL: So you couldn't have done that at Manzanar?
YK: Oh, no. So then we saw the movies. I guess it ended about, probably after ten, and so we'd go back to the -- oh, before we went to the movie, though, we had our shopping list. Neighbors, friends, families, all the things that we couldn't get in camp. So we'd go to the ten cent stores, buy bags full of things. And so we had all that shopping. When we got to camp it would be, oh, after eleven. Not too bad, but then the next morning we had to sort out the things for the family and friends.
AL: Did you get to have your own apartment there, or did you have to share it?
YK: In Heart Mountain, we shared, because my wife was going to give birth in the camp. '43 we had our first boy.
