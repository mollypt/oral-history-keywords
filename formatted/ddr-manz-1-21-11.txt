AL: This is Alisa Lynch with the Manzanar National Historic Site Oral History Project. Today is September 8, 2006, this is tape 2, side A of the interview with Yoshisuke Jack Kunitomi. So you were just talking about, we were talking about your neighborhood and your house. I wanted back up for just a second and have you talk a little bit about your memories of Amelia Street School. Did your whole family go to Amelia Street?
YK: Yes. Well, I don't remember very much about kindergarten, but what struck me as odd was the facilities that the school had. It was eight grades, kindergarten to eighth, because we didn't have junior high school. Okay, eighth grade, every morning when, after I became, oh, I guess, second, third, fourth grade, the custodian lady, female custodian would come to the room. "How many want to take a bath?" And all the Japanese hands went up because we were used to taking baths at home, at night. So we all took a bath again.
AL: So it wasn't because you needed it, it was because you enjoyed it?
YK: No, we... yeah. So we all took a bath again, which was nice, refreshed. And that was good, made us feel at home. But anyway, and then, as you got to the upper grades, fourth and fifth grade, we had a penny lunch, or cafeterias for people that, no one was home, so they ate at school. Well, we took our turns serving lunch for the, those who ate there, and that was, oh, that was my first money-making venture. Of course, we had to finish up the lunch, we were given free lunch, but we were given lunch to eat, and if you didn't finish lunch, you lost your privilege of working at lunch line. One of my friends didn't like milk, so he said, "I'll give you five cents to drink my milk," and boy, I jumped at that.
AL: Five cents a day?
YK: Five cents for whatever we had. Not, not in those cartons, but cups, they poured out of the big old quart jars. So he slipped me five dollars and I would drink his milk.
AL: Five, you mean five cents.
YK: Five cents. So I have money for candy or whatever, after school.
AL: That must have been why you were strong that you could bicycle so far, all that milk. [Laughs]
YK: [Laughs] Oh, yeah. But he and I had a good relationship.
