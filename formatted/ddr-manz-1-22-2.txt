AL: Who was, who was your best friend in grammar school?
YK: My friend who gave me a nickel for a cup of milk to drink. He was our neighbor from around the corner, and we'd always been close because he was just a year younger than I was.
AL: What's his name?
YK: Frank Sugiyama. And I couldn't... well, later, in a few years, his younger brother joined the Boy Scouts, and he was learning to play the bugle, and he would practice Saturday mornings, practicing on his bugle, the different notes, and kept us awake. And we had to ask him, "Please, don't start so early in the morning," to practice his notes. But we were all in the same club outside of school, so we enjoyed each other's company.
AL: Were you in the Boy Scouts?
YK: No, I never joined... somehow the Boy Scouts started just, just after I had become a ninth grader, and well, we were too old. They did not have the Explorer Scout at that time yet, so my friend's, best friend's younger brother was of age when he was in upper grades. So that group, the whole group took over, joined the Koyasan Buddhist Temple, and they formed their drum & bugle corps, which became city famous, California famous. And they marched nationwide in Washington, D.C., they did so much for our city, our local Boy Scouts.
AL: Were any of your brothers in the Boy Scouts, your younger brothers?
YK: Yes, they did join.
AL: Which ones?
YK: I think Tetsu did. So back to our high school, we started ninth grade, we were scrubs, teased by the seniors, older brothers and such. But what made our high school unique was we were there with the Italians. Lincoln High School on North Broadway had, population was half Italian. They were on the verge of moving from North, North Broadway, around Elysian Park, just north of our present-day Chinatown. So our football teams are mixed with Italians, Chinese, Japanese, very few African Americans, very few Spanish-speaking.
