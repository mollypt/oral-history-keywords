AL: Is that the East-West Player's theater?
YK: Yes. And those days, construction sites weren't closed. When the workers left at five, they just left everything. Well, we didn't steal any tool, well, shovels and hoes to mix concrete and things like that. So they were building, they dug a big hole for that gymnasium in the back of the building. We knew it was a gymnasium because it was just bare floor, not, not cemented or wooden. So we were playing there after the workmen left, and they had, were making a small kitchen. So we dug into the wall, never thinking of landslides and cave-ins. We were digging into the wall, it was adobe, so it was not liable to cave in easily, I don't think. So we dug in and finally, workmen got finished with the work and would see scars of our digging with regular shovels or whatever, left by the workmen. And so we left our memories there. And next, in the next few months, the next building was built, that San Pedro firm building. They had the original building still there.
AL: Is that First and San Pedro?
YK: No, not, just a few lots north of... the church is, you know the church, and the other one is a, sort of a brick, square... three stories, four stories? It was going to be office building on the bottom floor, and apartments in the top floors. So those, well, the buildings are original, too, still on the corner of First and San Pedro. So then talking about building, the [inaudible] old museum was that next building to come up, and it came up on Central or First Street. And we had fun there because, for one thing, I don't know who was taking care of the building, but they would leave caskets in the... well, I guess it was a, I don't know, family room.
AL: Caskets?
YK: Yeah, casket, bodies, a dead body there. So we used to scare people and say, "Hey, let's go look at the bodies," and on some nights when there were services, they would have open casket for people, family to pay their last respects. But that came up and took over another place for the young people to assemble.
AL: So were you attending the services and seeing the people in the caskets, or were you sneaking in after hours and looking at 'em?

YK: Yeah. And one thing that, talking about casket, Fukui Mortuary used to do a lot of that. They had a room where they prepared the bodies. My younger sister Sue and Midori were very close to the Fukuis' daughters, and they would play there, and they would, of course, scare each other. The girls' family was, you know, "Come here, I want to show you something," and show the girls the casket and the girl. And so they were getting scared out of their wits. [Laughs]
