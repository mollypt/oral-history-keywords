AL: What was your response to leaving Rohwer as a kid?
TO: You know, even though you had this whole aura of, you know, what the adults were feeling and thinking and saying, you know, I have to admit that, for my age, we were having a lot of fun. As I said, we chased bees and [inaudible]. When I spoke before the National Park Service, I said that as a city girl, I probably would not have come be an outdoor lover but for my experience there, going out to the swamps, and taking hikes and walks and then being in Elko where there's snow and deserts and all kinds of creatures and animals. It was a good time, it was a good time.
AL: When you talk about being outside the camp and wandering around, was the camp, was there like a barbed-wire fence around the camp, guard towers?
TO: Yes, there was. But towards the end, I think that there was, they were a lot more liberal about letting people just move out.

TO: Well, I knew they were there, but no, I didn't have any interaction, you'd say, like hey, that's Mike or so-and-so, there wasn't any conversation, friendships, but I...

TO: Uh-huh. AL: Were these predominately black people, or whites?
TO: White people, although I have to tell you, when I went to Little Rock with the president, this was the Fortieth Anniversary of the desegregation case, Central High School. And Ozell told me that he grew up in Gould, and Gould was just right outside of Rohwer, and he's African American, and all he said is, "We were po'." He said, "When you're really poor, you're po'." But anyway, the one person I'd like to mention when I went to Gould, is I went with a Japanese American whose father decided to remain in Little Rock after they were released, and raised his family there, and so this gentleman I was introduced to by some of the university types that I met during the celebration, accompanied me out there and then he called Rosalie Gould, and Rosalie is this lady, magnificent woman, I don't know if you met her at the Rohwer reunion. The reunion where I was the dinner keynote speaker, she was there being recognized and that was probably, every two years, we just did one...
