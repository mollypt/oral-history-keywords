AL: Speaking of marriage, when did you meet Masa, your wife?
YK: Well, I was at the Los Angeles Junior College for three years because we had no, really, major to speak of. But in the meantime, in the mid-'30s, that community was very active. Girls sports was just beginning to start, softball and basketball. The men's were very active, baseball, softball, basketball, they were very heavy on those three sports. So, and other social sports like roller skating. Roller skating was very popular for the girls clubs. There were, of course, many girls clubs in high school, Japanese club and Japanese club cultural, they would dress in kimonos, or Japanese Girls Day had kimonos, and it was celebrated, Girls Day and so forth, and it was a big thing. So in the community itself, girls would also continue high school activity in the community, and they want to have parties, social, dancing. So the girls clubs were a big, becoming very active. Mas's club at Hollywood High School, smaller and Japanese Americans, but as I recall, Queen Esther's name from the Bible. And so it was affiliated with a Presbyterian church in Highland -- Hollywood, I remember Highland Avenue. Well, there were, the girls club would sponsor skating party, and skating parties were held at Lincoln Park, the Shrine Auditorium, in Culver City, many different places. The girls club would sponsor them, and they would charge thirty-five cents a ticket, which meant that the skating, skating company would probably get twenty-five cents, and the sponsoring girls club would get ten cents. So we'd get skates, and they would have men's, men's only skating, ladies only, couples only, and free for all. And so when the boys go out skating and the girls are around, you're skating in and out, flirting, and you're showing off, and that's how it happened. You're flirting with all the strange girls, and Masa, my future wife, would come with her sisters. She had four other sisters, and she would come with her brother, older brother. And well, we'd get acquainted somehow, and through the years, plus going to the ballgames, softball games, to watch the girls play softball, and you get acquainted that way. So it was just a matter of flirting with the girls, [inaudible], meeting the brothers. Brothers you meet playing each other, too, softball, basketball. So when you get to know the brothers, then you get to know the sisters. [Laughs]
