AL: What did you do, what was your job at Heart Mountain?
YK: Well, I met a friend that had a nice, soft job. All we did was check the inventory of the warehouses of certain supplies, some food supplies. So we had a pencil and a pad and a list, count whatever cases that came in, verify the count with somebody else's inventory, and that's what we did. We were called counters. [Laughs]
AL: And did Masa have a job at Heart Mountain?
YK: She went as a hospital...
AL: Oh, so she worked at the hospital at Manzanar and at Heart Mountain?
YK: Yes. She just happened to know a doctor, doctor that, the father of a older, older sister's future husband.
AL: How did you, how did you get your job on the newspaper? What did you do and how did you get that job?
YK: Well, I don't know, newspaper was a weekly, and since I was heavy into sports, lot of my friends were coaching. Every so often, people would leave on the furlough, weekly furlough, farm, agricultural furlough, or a long, long furlough to schools back east, there would be vacancies on the staff like newspaper. So there would be no one with a byline. So sports editor was open, no byline for the column, weekly column. So one day I just asked if there was an opening and said, "Yeah." Got to be the managing editor.
AL: For the sports page?
YK: Yes.
AL: Were sports as important at Heart Mountain as they were in Manzanar?
YK: Oh, yes.
AL: What kind of sports were people doing?
YK: Well, again, basketball in the winter, hardball in the summer, and because Heart Mountain sits in Wyoming, where there are many smaller towns, Lovell, Cody, Thermopolis, and they are all into basketball. So now, they started having inter-, well, camp rivalry games.
AL: With the local people?
YK: Yes, local. So they would have... because they were not in California, our team could travel to their high school to play. Whereas in Arizona where the camp team played out of town Arizona team and beat them, they could not go out to play against them on, because they were still too close to the West Coast. In fact, they, they came from, Gila, Arizona, came to Heart Mountain to play baseball.
AL: How did that happen?
YK: [Shrugs] I was out of camp and I was in the service. I read about that, and I said, "Oh, that was great."
AL: How did, how did the local people treat you, the non-Japanese up in Wyoming?
YK: Well, I guess, I know my brother-in-law who played football...
AL: Is that Ted?
YK: No, the younger, younger one. He was on the Heart Mountain football team. They played... all they said was they were, they were too big, size-wise, physically. Because in basketball, although they kind of held their own, they said, well, the boys were really much bigger than the camp boys.
