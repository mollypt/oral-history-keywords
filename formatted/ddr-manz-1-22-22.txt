AL: And so when they, when they took you back in, where did they send you?
YK: From Denver, we were sent to Camp Blanding, Florida. Florida, that was a big IRTC, Infantry Replacement Training Center. It was a huge, huge camp. So we went on a troop train, open on the side, bunk beds showing, and we went for a week, I think. Denver, Arkansas, Nebraska, Missouri, Tennessee, parts of Kentucky, parts of Georgia, parts of Florida. And we got to [inaudible] old, old South. And by golly, when you say the deep South, when you saw it at that time, you saw that Jim Crow.
AL: What did you see?
YK: Well, shacks. Shacks in the hills there, in the valleys, slums. People living in poverty. And then when you got to Florida, more of the same. But...
AL: Were you, when you got to Florida, were you segregated with other Japanese Americans or were you in with everybody?
YK: Well, our training battalion was all Japanese. Some have come from different units.
AL: What was the battalion, do you remember the battalion number?
YK: 232nd Infantry Battalion.
AL: And so were you expecting to go to Europe?
YK: Oh, yes. We were the replacements.
AL: So how did you end up in the MIS?
YK: Well, after seventeen weeks of basic, all the weapons and whatever, hiking in the swamps and things like that, we were on the last few days of the seventeen weeks, we had a meeting and we were told, "Wait here for sergeant, 2nd lieu from Camp Savage," yeah. They were from Camp Savage originally, in Minneapolis. So he came to interview us, we were all in a day room. So we lined up, our sarges would call out the name, we'd go up to talk to the sergeant from Minneapolis, and said, "Can you read this Japanese reader?" I read a few Chinese characters, "Okay over there," "You, over there." So we were divided. So two of us were picked to go to language school.
AL: What did you think of that?
YK: Well, I said, well, I was older than the majority of the other soldiers there, so since I had a family, I had a sigh of relief.
AL: Was Dale already born before you left?
YK: Yes.
AL: So you went to Camp Savage, how long were you there?
YK: Let's see. So two, about two years, I think. We had, it's all language, and we're learning the military language of the Japanese army. So we went to geography, history, and we went to, I remember played basketball, played baseball. So we had a whole season of sports there. Then it came to the day... we are ready to, we didn't have graduation, we were all ready to be shipped out. So the whole battalion shipped out en masse because it seemed the whole train was our graduating class.
AL: Where did they send you?
YK: So we went from Florida, and of course, still blinds were, blinded, window blinds. We ended up in San Pedro, California, where we started from. [Laughs] And we, well, some places we were able to see, we came through San Bernardino, [inaudible], San Bernardino. Came through there, South Gate, Huntington Park, on our way to San Pedro. So we came through, San Bernardino through, I guess Compton. So we ended up at San Pedro, and people said, "Hey, we're home now." [Laughs]
