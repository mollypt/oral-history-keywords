JA: Tell me a little bit about school. You were... what grade of school were you in?
AM: Let's see. I finished my tenth grade in Roosevelt High School here in Los Angeles, so I started from the 11th grade. I remember the first day we went, when they were trying to start the school we had to just attend, not for teaching or anything but just to register and things. We went into the classroom and there was no chairs or anything, just an empty room, so we all sat on the floor and registered and things like that. Eventually they were able to find some used desks and things like that and they were brought into camp. And some of the tables were built in camp, and chairs.
JA: Was it adequate equipment for things like science courses?
AM: Well, I think for science and things like that it was pretty hard, I think, yeah. I didn't take science, but there was a science class, though.
JA: Did they teach civics and things like, about...
AM: Yes, uh-huh. I don't know how, it must have been difficult for the teacher to talk about democracy when we were put into a place like that, but they had regular, you know, classes. I remember there was one teacher there who taught public speaking, his name was Clive Greenlee. He was a blind teacher, but he came to Manzanar and taught public speaking. I had him as one of my teachers, and he would have to depend on his dog to go places. But then the dog was really confused because there was no sidewalk, you see, so he had a hard time. So some of the other teachers would help this Mr. Greenlee go from one place to another. He was a real nice teacher.
