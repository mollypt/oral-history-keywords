AL: This is Alisa Lynch on Friday, September 8, 2006, at the home of Yoshisuke "Jack" Kunitomi at 424 South Lincoln Avenue in Monterey Park, Apartment A. Jack is a former internee of Manzanar and Heart Mountain, and a member of the NIS -- excuse me, MIS. He'll be talking about his experiences. This interview is being conducted for the oral history program at Manzanar National Historic Site in Independence, California, and will be archived in the site library. And do we have your permission, Jack, to use the interview for educational purposes?
YK: Yes, you may.
AL: Okay. When and where were you born?
YK: Way back in October 10, 1915. I was born on North Central Avenue, which is across the street from our veterans memorial, right north of our museum. 155 North Central Avenue is what is on my birth certificate.
AL: So you were born at home?
YK: Yes. In those days, there were many midwives taking care of the births. And oh, Japanese town was full of them. In fact, our neighbor, she was not the one to deliver me, but there were many right in Japanese town, and they went house to house because families were getting settled, and people were having children.
AL: What is your, what is your given name?
YK: My name given name is Yoshisuke.
AL: How do you spell that?
YK: Y-O-S-H-I-S-U-K-E.
AL: And what does that mean?
YK: That's, that's the thing that worried me all these years. "Yoshi" means well-done, something that's good, well-done. And "suke" is the suffix added to names, the old-fashioned names. And I don't know really what it means, but I looked up my last name, Kunitomi, because the Chinese characters define it as a place where the natural resources are plenty, very plentiful, very, like a rich soil, something like that. And when I saw that, I said, "Oh, I must be a wealthy, full of natural resources." [Laughs]
AL: You are. [Laughs]
