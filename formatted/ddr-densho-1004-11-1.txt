gky: And Grant just celebrated his eighty-first birthday. He was born on November 9th in the year 1919. Grant, what were the circumstances of you going to Japan and studying?
GH: As a child, there was three of us, same grade, same age, same height, and two of them went to Japan during summer vacation. And they came back, and one day while we were fishing, the fishing was very poor so the conversation shifted to their experience in Japan. And I was listening. I was an outsider because what they were telling me was quite strange. For instance, they said, "Do you know they drive on the wrong side of the street, that you have to take off your shoes to get into the house?" And it did arouse my curiosity. So I asked my father, "Dad, I would like to go to Japan, too." And my one-on-one with my father was usually when I got a haircut. And every time I got a haircut, I would tell Dad, "I want to go to Japan." And my father's response was, "Son," he said, "There's eight of you. I can't send eight of you to Japan on summer vacation." But I was very persistent, and one day I said, "Dad, if you're not going to send me I'm going to swim across the Pacific Ocean." That impressed my dad. He said, "If you're that determined," he said, "Well, I'll have to think about it." He said, "It would be worth your trip if you were to study in Japan and," he said, "Perhaps two years." And I said... I agreed. That is how I got to Japan.
gky: You wound up staying longer than two years.
GH: Yes. I did manage to get into the Japanese middle school atchuugakkou. And when I did enterchuugakkou, it was very difficult and I was ready to come home, and I said "Dad, I have fulfilled my two years, I would like to come home." My father said, "You finish high school." So I did stay for the period of eight years.
gky: So, until you were twenty.
GH: All together, yes.
gky: What did you study inchuugakkou?
GH: Well, inchuugakkou, same as our high -- it's very comparable to our high school, of course, with the exception that you do have compulsory military training, which is five years.
gky: Why don't you talk a little but about what you do in that military training. You know, what kind of weapons you...
GH: Yeah. Well, the first three years it's close order drill type and manual review. But the fourth and fifth grader, they were issued a gun. And the fourth and fifth graders shared a gun as well as gas masks. During my fifth year, I was even issued a saber. During our fourth and fifth year, we did receive training, something comparable to recruitment, if not more, because we did actually have those light machine guns, knee mortars. I actually went through tear gas training with a gas mask. So I would say if they had to take us to the front line, we were very much prepared to do so.

GH: Well, as a student, I did a march, a victory march, etcetera, but at all times I knew I was an American.

But my grades were still wanting, so I was debating how I would enter the university. But Uncle Sam solved that problem because I received a draft notice.
