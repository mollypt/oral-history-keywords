GH: The other occasion was we were deep behind enemy lines and I was suffering because if you eat, you got to go. If you go, I have to double-time to catch up with the troops and it seemed like every time I catch up, the ten-minute break is over. So I'm constantly on the move. The medical officer realized the shape I was in, took my pack and put it on a horse. So I followed the horse until the horse was shot. It was moved to a mule, but the mule, the load shifted and it had a sore so bad that it had to be destroyed. Then I was on another mule, my pack was on another mule. So I would always follow the mule, and when the going got tough I would hang onto the mule's tail. And I can still remember the mule looking back as if to say, "Give me a break." [Laughs] One day, the medical officer said, "Grant, you're in pretty bad shape." I said, "Well, I'm hanging in there." But he said, he reminded me that they didn't have -- they were carrying buckets of silver rupee, and then he didn't say that I should take advantage of that, but what he was telling me that they would -- I could stay behind with the native Kachin and that they would give several pieces to natives. And I said to the Captain, "What happens after the war?" He said, "You will have to find your way back." I says, "Thanks, but no thanks. I'm going to hang on to the mule's tail." [Laughs]

gky: Now tell me that story about -- so your pack is on this third mule...

gky: ...and you have a ten-minute break. So can you tell me that story?
GH: Yes. During the ten-minute break, well, this is the longest two minutes of my life. The mule just collapsed, and when the ten-minute break was up, I looked and I could see the men walking and when they disappeared around the bend, I said, "Oh, my gosh." I said, for me to unload the pack and carry the pack and double-time it to catch up, it was almost impossible. And I tried to help by pulling the mule's tail, but that didn't help. Finally, the mule skinner, he went down all the list of the curse words he knew, and then he made a big pull and said, "Damn it, you volunteered, too," and with that the mule rose. [Laughs] And that's how I survived.
