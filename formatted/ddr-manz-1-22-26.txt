AL: How long were you in Japan?
YK: Oh, about two years.
AL: Did you ever get to go to Okayama and see your family?
YK: Oh, yes, oh, yes. My friend, friend's friend left, well, my friend's friend was hiding in a south prefecture because even though he was a citizen of America, American citizen, he had been forced to join the Japanese army. Because if you do that, you lose your American citizenship, that's what I was told. So this fellow went hiding toward Okinawa or someplace to get, escape the MPs. But that time, the MPs was in charge, Australian army was in charge of the southern part of Japan, and they gradually gave that over to the American army, Eighth Army. So Jumbo told him to come back, says he was safe. So in the meantime, he had looked up the address of my relatives, so he had the address and the way to get there.
AL: What was it like to meet them?
YK: Well, I barely communicated, but I could see from the facial feature that they looked --
[Interruption]
AL: You were talking about meeting your family in Japan?
YK: The first lady I saw coming down the road was quite tall. And I, when I looked at her closely, she looked exactly like my younger sister, Midori. So I said, I called her by her, my sister's name, and she said, "Oh, no, I'm Yuri." So she looked so much like my sister that then I realized I was in Japan with the family. And so I met, I met the family and the head man of the homestead, and I had someyens, black marketyens, which I gave to them. And of course they were grateful, but being farmers, they didn't really need money, 'cause they can exchange things for, farm fruit for money or whatever. So I was back there several times with my friend, Jumbo.
AL: Did you get to see Kimbo, your brother Kimbo when you were in Japan?
YK: No, no. I never, I never saw him down there. He was in the northern part of Japan with the paratroopers.
AL: Were you in Japan when Ted Fujioka was killed?
YK: No, because I heard that news in Minneapolis. That was November, about, '44? Yes.
AL: Did, did Masa stay at Heart Mountain through the whole war?
YK: Yes. We were able to call the wives because the wives of the service people were housed in a place called a hostel, they have hostels in many eastern cities. So she was in the hostel, and that's where she was.
AL: Where did she go when the war was over? Did she go with her family?
YK: After I left, the brother, older brother came from Heart Mountain and took them back to camp. So they were in camp together at the end, and then they came back to Hollywood.
