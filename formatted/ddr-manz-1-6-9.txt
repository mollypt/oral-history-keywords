JA: What kind of extracurricular activities did you get involved with? Sports, or...
AM: Well, I played some baseball, a little basketball. Well, I used to sneak out of camp and go fishing. [Laughs] And that was really a nice feeling, you leave about seven o'clock, after it's dark, and you go through the barbed wire and walk along the stream and go fishing and you walk all the way to the foothill and it's about two o'clock in the morning, you sleep a little bit, and then you start fishing downstream, and by the time you come down near the camp it's about five or six o'clock in the morning, and then the feeling was... it's hard to describe the feeling, that you got away with it, you know. "See, I was able to fish and I didn't get caught and they didn't even..." [laughs]. Well, that was quite a satisfaction and then, of course, those days, because of the limit of the gasoline, not too many people went fishing along the streams, so boy, we used to catch a lot.
JA: And then did you cook 'em up at home?
AM: Yes, uh-huh. We'd take it to the mess hall and have the cook cook it for us, let him have some, give him some and then he'll cook it for us.
JA: And nobody caught on?
AM: No. [Laughs] They wanted to eat the fish, so they're not gonna lose the source of all the fishes.
JA: You must've been tired the next day.
AM: Oh, yeah. [Laughs]
