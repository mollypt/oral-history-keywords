JA: What was yours and his and your family's thoughts when you were finally told you had to go away to camp?
AM: Well, you know, I think you must have heard that word, Japanese word called, they'd say,shikata ga nai. You know, there's nothing we can do about it so we just have to do whatever we can.Shikata ga naimeans it can't be helped, can't do anything about it, you know. So, my father naturally packed everything and put everything into storage, and of course I had a camera which, I had a little Browning camera, I took it to the police station and turned it in. But, like I said before, he kept one lens and some film holder already at that time when he was supposed to have turned it in. Of course, his thought was, "Okay, what's a lens without a body to it?" so if he got caught he says it's only a lens, it's not a camera, he had that kind of thinking in his mind. So, he wasn't too concerned about being arrested. But, I think... he must have had a special permission to take these photographs for identification purpose and things.
AL: Can you ask him, one, to address the curfew? He was talking about the 5-mile, but just the fact that there was a curfew. And secondly, he told me before that they were photographing a wedding on December 7th, and guys were being hauled out in tuxedos.
JA: That's interesting. Yeah, she's saying, could you tell us a little bit more about the curfew along with the 5-mile limit?
AM: Well, let's see. I forgot when the thing came into act, but we were ordered, you know, to... all the camera things, cameras and arms and shortwave radios were considered contraband for the Japanese American and that we were supposed to stay within 5-mile radius from where you lived.
JA: Were there time limits, too, as to when you could be out?
AM: I can't remember that. There could have been a time limit, but I can't remember what it was.
JA: Alisa mentioned that, was it December 7th, you were photographing a wedding?
AM: Oh, my father was, had a wedding that day. In fact, he had a few appointments in the studio, family pictures and things like that, and then that afternoon he had a wedding. The wedding went off okay, and then at the reception, there must've been a couple hundred people there, and then right in the middle of the reception, FBI came and arrested some of the people. They were still in their tuxedo and things and they were just taken away that way. I don't know where they went, but I know my father-in-law, he lived in San Fernando, and he got arrested and he was in Tujunga jail. So they went to different parts of the city jail, I don't know which one, but there was one in Terminal Island, too, some of the people were put in jail over there because they were allowed to have a visitor eventually and some other people went to see them there at Terminal Island.
