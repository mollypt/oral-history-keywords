SE: My mother came from a family that was quite well-to-do in Japan and she had an older brother and a sister. But in Japanese custom generally, the eldest have to stay home and take charge of the family business. But the oldest one was a brother, and much as he wanted to go to college, he couldn't do it because the family tradition made it that he had to stay home. And my mother was her dad's favorite, and she was the youngest, but she was very curious about things. So she wanted to go to college, and so she got to go to the, I think it's called Aoyama Women's college in Tokyo, and it's very unusual at that time for girls to be able to get college education. But she got her way and she went to Tokyo to the school. Day that they were packing for her to leave for college, her brother's wife, with tears in her eyes, 'cause she wanted her husband to be able to go to school, she packed her bag for her, and then she put a long rope in her bag, and she said, "Now, be sure, if there is a fire in the dormitory and you're in the upstairs room, be sure to tie this rope to something very secure and let yourself down out of the window. And so she was kind and thoughtful when my mother was able to go to school. When she finished college, she had read all about America and all the wonderful things that were available in this country, so she told her dad that she wanted to go to America. Well, I think there were lots of requests for "picture brides" from the United States because the young men that came over came as a young man, maybe they were still teenagers, and got jobs in the States. And when they had enough money saved, they sent for the wives. And so my father was one of three brothers that were in Seattle, and there was a family of three girls in this, it was the same prefecture or state in Japan. And so the oldest brother got the oldest daughter, and then my father was supposed to marry the middle one, and the other brother married the youngest. But my father was, what do you call it, a renegade. He didn't want to go the way his brothers went, so he opted to get his own bride. And so my mother came from Japan with a Japanese minister, and it's strange, but it had the same surname as the husband-to-be. And tearfully, her father packed the most beautiful Japanese gowns that he could for her, and sent her with the Japanese instrument, thekoto, and he sent all kinds of things with her and they came. And here it was, she came to marry a poor laborer that didn't have any money or anything other than what he could just barely give her. So she said for many days at first she wore her Japanesekimono, and then her sister-in-laws all scoffed at her that "nobody dresses like that in this country." And so she put all her beautifulkimonosaway in the trunk and dressed like the rest of them. They were married in a living room of a friend of my father's, and I don't know if this minister that brought her married them or not. That part I don't know. But that was in... what was it, 1909, I think, and it's sometime in August in 1909.
