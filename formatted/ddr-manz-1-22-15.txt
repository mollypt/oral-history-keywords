AL: What, what was life like in those early days of Manzanar? I mean, like, where did you use the bathroom, where did you eat, what did you do during the day?
YK: Well, everyone says there was no privacy in that camp. First of all, there were twenty-four barracks a block, and the walls were just plain wood, so arguments, crying, fighting, were not private affairs. Each block had a mess hall, each block had a laundry room together with the shower facilities. And well, as I said, bathroom had no privacy. So you had sensitive people going to bathrooms, shower, late at night, early in the morning, to avoid being with other people. But most of us were working on camouflage nets. Somehow they had the nets strung up on a high beam, straight across, 20 feet or so across, and with teams of people stringing the camouflage pieces through the nets. Of course, we were paid, what, twelve dollars a month, that was the going pay for unskilled labor.
AL: What was your job at the net factory?
YK: Well, you have a strip of burlap, two and half by, oh, I thought they came about six foot lengths. So you put it in and out in a certain pattern. And I don't know how long it took to finish one net, it was strung up so high that oh, probably... oh, I couldn't hazard a guess. Twenty feet.
AL: What were the nets used for?
YK: Well, to camouflage, like, tanks, when you're shelling a hill or other background. Shelling some big gun emplacements.
