AL: You've told me some stories before about the black market and some of the adventures that you had in Japan. Can you share any of those?
YK: Well, I had a friend who was stranded in Japan. He had gone to Japan earlier.
AL: What's his name?
YK: Well, we call him Jumbo. Jumbo James Mochizuki. He had gone to Japan because his father had retired from Japan. He tried to come back to America before the crisis exploded, but the ship just left port and turned around and went back. So English-speaking, Japanese face, very knowledgeable about Japan. He found a job in the interpretive section. But all the social hours, we went to taxi dance hall, and they were running them not in Tokyo because Tokyo was flattened, in the suburbs of Tokyo, especially in the city of Kawasaki. Knowing some of the dance hall operators, Jumbo promised them PX goods. So we went to the PX, bought cigarettes, chocolate, what have you, and took them and gave them to the band players and hangers-on. So we made a little side money on that. We did nothing big because...
AL: Was that illegal?
YK: Oh, of course it is illegal, but we didn't do anything big because we were, there were too many MPs around. I know friends that got caught up in the web of making bigger, bigger money.
AL: What about the coffee grounds? Wasn't there a story about the coffee grounds?
YK: Oh, yes. My friend, same friend, he was living at the NYK building, which housed all the interpreters. And being very glib in Japanese, he got along with the Japanese employees. So jokingly, he said, "I can get you coffee." They said, "How?" Said, "Well, from our American mess hall you can get, use the mess hall." Dry them upstairs and use them as fresh coffee, which they thought was a fabulous idea. So we got in, he got into the market of recycling GI coffee beans, grounds, for real coffee. And so they were able to eke out a living from that.
