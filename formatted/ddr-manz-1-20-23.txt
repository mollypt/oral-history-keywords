AL: What characteristics, I mean, looking at your parents now, what characteristics would you say are most like, where you're most like your father? And what characteristics would you say that you got from your mom?
TO: That's a wonderful question. 'Cause some of these things, as I said, I was reflecting and thanking them at the cemetery on Mothers' Day. My father's an only child, so he liked to spend time by himself, and he was not lonely. He enjoys being alone. And I'm like him, in that I like to read, and spend time alone. And he was always learning something, learning wasn't something that took place in a university or school, and he was always learning new things. And I thank him for that, because I do that. One problem is, finding place for books. I have hid 'em all over. [Laughs] My mother -- and there's some other things I would say about my dad. But my mother, and I'll show you these pictures later, is, we're poor. We are poor, and yet you look at these pictures, and this was in the '40s, and she's just this elegant woman. Has her hair up like Esther Williams, or in a pompadour like Ivanda Carlo, with the, with the shoulder pads and everything, and she just, she was just, just gorgeous. What I, what I picked up from her is, I was a funny-looking little thing, and you'll see also, but that she... I thank her because pretty women sometimes are not very kind to not-pretty daughters. I think my mother always looked at me like I was perfect. And probably because I was strong and smart and healthy and, you know, I was just a good kid. So she was not pretty. [Laughs] But what I like most about her, where I think I'm like her, is that she did things for her family. My mother would sew all day in a factory, I think at one point where maybe the most she made was $1,500 a year doing piecework. And here... I want you to know this, this woman is a daughter of a medical doctor, and from a nice family, and she had all kind of maids and all, and now here she is, sewing.
AL: This was after the war?
TO: Yeah, and taking in sewing and... but, no matter how late, if it's going to be something special, Easter, Christmas, whatever, she'd make me a pretty dress, and there are just many, many nights she used to stay up late to make certain that I was going to have a dress. But I think how I'm most like my mother is my mother likes to make people happy. And I just feel really lucky because she was just a nice person, a good person, and maybe she did mourn her situation, having a very privileged childhood, and then the struggles she had, but she didn't ever appear to be a whiner or anything, and I pick up a lot of that from her, 'cause... and trying to do good for other people, that's the way she was. My dad was much more of a solitary guy. So interesting, my husband is like my dad a lot. He enjoys spending time by himself and the kinds of things, if you play, you're a fly fisherman, that's not like going out with the guys in a boat, and drinking beer, you know. And he does jewelry work, art work, those are solitary, solo activities. He used to play the guitar and whatever else. Tommy is like my dad. My mom's gregarious, and in the end, I think what I love most about my parents is that they, before they passed on, they had a lot of time to do creative things. So there was painting, singing, dancing, they both went ballroom dancing. My mother would do some of the folk dancing for Nisei Week, she and all her little [inaudible] gals and all. So, but I think my mom really had a, kind of a [inaudible] and that she was gonna be positive, no matter what. And that's what I remember most. And that can't be the experience, you know, because a lot of it is just the strength and the character and the background, but, 'cause it was a devastating experience, but my mom was always positive, and so that's what we heard. They spared us of the negatives and the worries.
