AL: Going   back to leaving Rohwer, did you go, when you left Rohwer, did you   go on a train, come back to California via train?
TO: Yes,   to Elko. Elko, from Elko was another trip, after my parents   returned from San Francisco and successfully overturning the   deportation orders, they came to take us to Los Angeles. And I'm   sure he had several trips to Los Angeles to try to figure out where   we could stay. And one of his, we call them uncles, I guess they   found a place, and that's where we moved in and then my father's   cousin moved in with his family as well. So we were pretty crowded   in this little house in East L.A.
AL: You   were in Elko, you said, two years, is that right?
TO: Maybe   not that long.
AL: Did   your father get that deportation order while he was still at   Rohwer, or was it when he came back to the Coast?
TO: No, it   must have been while we were still at Rohwer, because we stayed   'til the end and then we ended up in Elko. But our trip from Elko,   my dad bought a car, and now we were in the Mojave Desert, and we   had a flat tire. And he takes the tire off, and these tires had   inner tubes, and the inner tube had so many patches you couldn't   even see the original inner tube. And I'm going to tell you, here's   this Japanese family in the middle of the desert, and fortunately,   you know, the old pioneer spirit about helping those in trouble   prevailed, because the first car that came by stopped, took the   tire into town, somebody fixed it, and then sometime later,   somebody dropped it off.
[Interruption]
AL: And we   were talking about when your family left Elko...
TO:   Right...
AL: ...and   the flat tire...
TO: Right.   So what, now, I'm only about seven years old, or something like   that?
AL: Seven   years old. Well, actually, maybe a little older, because you were,   been in Elko for a while.
TO: Going   into the third grade, about, I think.
AL: Okay.   So you were in school, though, in Elko, you said?
TO:   Yes.
AL:   Okay.
