JA: Your father was an acquaintance of Ansel Adams, is that right?
AM: Yes, he became acquainted with him when Ansel Adams came to Manzanar.
JA: Uh-huh.
AM: Now, Ansel Adams was a good friend of Edward Weston.
JA: And your father knew him.
AM: Yes.
JA: Tell me about that.
AM: Okay. Early in the 1920s when my father started getting interested in photography, he went to a couple of classes in photography and Edward Weston was one of his teachers, or he got taught about photography directly from Mr. Weston, and they became very good friends. And then he was, Edward Weston was living in Glendale at the time with his three sons, and he was really struggling to make a living. He was -- what he did mainly was photograph and he would try to sell it and he would put it up in gallery to sell his photograph and this... I guess it was beginning of the Depression era so the sale wasn't that good. So my father found out about his struggle, and at that time in the Japanese community there was a camera club, and they were very active, and they knew of Edward Weston, these people. So my father thought of an idea, maybe have an exhibit in the Japanese community. And so he asked this camera club if they would sponsor him, and they were more than happy to do it because they knew of his work. And after the exhibit, quite a few photographs were sold. In fact, Edward Weston, in his two-volume book, I forgot the title right now, anyway, he wrote in there saying that, "The Japanese people liked my photographs so much that a lot of them even borrowed money just to buy my photograph." He was very appreciative of it. And then when Edward Weston heard that Ansel Adams was going to come to Manzanar to photograph, I guess he told Ansel Adams about my father, so when he came, he came to see my father right away. In fact, he took a photograph of my, our family in our apartment, and after doing that, my father and Ansel Adams sat and talked, and then he saw a little toy piano my sister had, so he got that and he started playing a classical piece on there. And my father was -- he used, he used to be in an orchestra so he liked classical music, so he recognized the tune right away and asked him, you know, told him that he's such a good piano player. And that's when Ansel Adams told him, "Yeah, I was studying to become a concert pianist but I got interested in photography," so that's the reason why he could, he could play the piece.
JA: That's pretty interesting.
