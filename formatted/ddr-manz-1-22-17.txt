AL: Did, after that first season, did you go back to Manzanar?
YK: No, no. When were out, my wife had kept in touch with her family, and so we knew the father was home from Camp Missoula. He had kidney stone, and so sent home because they didn't know how to treat him. Well, he recovered and got well, and we knew he was recovering, so we asked for a transfer to Heart Mountain, because that's only across the Rockies from Idaho Falls to Heart Mountain.
AL: Was that a difficult decision for you?
YK: No, no.
AL: How was Heart Mountain different than Manzanar?
YK: Well, when I left Manzanar, they were still fumbling around.
AL: You said "still fumbling around"?
YK: Yes, like looking for guidance. Heart Mountain was much better organized in the sense that, well, it's now how long? Almost a year since Heart Mountain was open. So yes, I think Heart Mountain was, later, better organized because Manzanar had a December 7th riot, so-called riot. So I guess that broke up the...
AL: Do you know what caused the riot? What did you hear about it?
YK: We were in Heart Mountain when we heard that, because it, we were in camp before, before December 7th. Well, there were some cooks who claimed the Caucasian purchaser or buyers or whatever were using the coupons for their own family. But you know how rumors start, it goes on and on and people really believe in it. And so they had a confrontation with the administrator and it came to name-calling, things like that. Well, it made it bad because my brother, younger brother who had been in camp putting on the finishing touches, was next to the boy that got killed.
AL: Is that Hideo?
YK: Yeah, he was two boys away from the boy that was killed. So I guess it was a bad feeling permeated the crowd and... yeah.
AL: How did you hear about it?
YK: Well, we heard on the radio that there was a riot, and the radio said it was a riot because the Japanese were celebrating Pearl Harbor attack. [Laughs] Anyway...
