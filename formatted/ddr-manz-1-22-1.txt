AL: This is Alisa Lynch with   Yoshisuke "Jack" Kunitomi on November 9, 2006. We're at his home in   Monterey Park, California. This is a continuation of an oral   history interview we began September 8, 2006. And before we begin   again, Jack, I just wanted to make sure we have your permission to   use the interview for educational purposes at Manzanar.
YK: Fine, that's fine.
AL: Okay. When we last talked, I   think we ended when we were talking about grammar school. And I was   curious what you could tell me about your high school years, where   you went to high school and what year you graduated?
YK: Yes, our elementary school was   located right, block north of our future store that we had taken   over. It was K-8, there was no middle, junior high school and   district at that time. So we went to K to 8. Our elementary school   was unique in that the upper grades had almost like an   occupational-oriented school. For the boys we had shoe repairing,   woodworking, called sloid. Why, it was co-educational, too, that   woodworking, because I remember we all worked on an annual regatta.   We would build our own sailboat, took them to the park, and   sponsored by Herald Express, afternoon Hearst paper. And   incidentally, I won one heat in their preliminary regatta and got a   blue ribbon. But for the ladies, we had home, home economics,   basket, basket weaving, and there was another thing, I've forgotten   what that was for the girls.
AL: Is this at Amelia Street   School?
YK: Yes, it was mostly, oh, I would   think fifth, sixth, seventh, eighth, eighth grade, upper grades.   And we had what we called the penny lunch, where the upper grade   people served lunch for those who couldn't go up to eat. And that's   where I made my money, first money in elementary school, where I   drank somebody's milk, glass of milk for him, because he couldn't   take milk, lactose intolerance. So I was rich, big spender at that   time.
AL: Who was your -- I'm sorry, go   ahead.
YK: Our teachers were notable in   that they cared for us because our fourth grade teacher, a Miss   Mauch, M-A-U-C-H, took it upon herself to accompany us on   Saturdays, on a field trip. And one notable place that we went to   was in Eagle Rock, just... just west of Pasadena. We took a   streetcar, of course we had parents' permission. I don't know who   forged my mother's signature on the permit, but we were able to go,   and we brought our lunches, paid streetcar fare, went, hiked up the   face of Eagle Rock, which somehow eroded into an eagle with   outspread wings. That was the face of it, and it was sheer cliff so   we couldn't find any place to picnic there. Well, we went behind   the cliff and around the block, and a large cave. So we had picnics   back there. Well, we spent our days running around the hills   chasing each other, and had a great time. So Miss Mauch, I remember   her.
Another fifth grade teacher was Miss   McDougall, she's probably Irish descent. She loved to sing, and   serenaded us because Japanese American boys were, we were shy. I   don't know, we didn't have the guts to perform. But we followed her   lead in singing from the district songbooks, and they were full of   patriotic song: [inaudible], what else? Oh, so many songs that were   banned by the district in the '60s, '70s. We couldn't even sing Battle Hymn of the Republic, which was one of our favorites.   So our upper grades in the elementary school are full of things   that, oh, things that we did, outside activities, starting playing   games, intramural games.
Came our graduation day, and of   course, our parents were so shy, they wouldn't come out to watch us   graduate. Our graduation was not that elaborate like now. We were   handed our diplomas, no cap and gown for the elementary.
AH: Were your parents involved at   all in your schooling?
YK: Not much, not much. They became   more involved when the girls were upper grades. But they were now   going to junior high school when Sue and Midori were graduating   elementary school, it was the sixth grade graduation. So the   mothers and fathers were more active somehow, being the same   gender, I guess.
