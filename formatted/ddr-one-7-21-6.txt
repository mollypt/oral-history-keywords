BB: Let's talk about your family and grandchildren now. We will take a look at a map later. Could you tell us about your grandchildren?
MO: I have four grandchildren here at the tofu store. They are all girls. The eldest three already graduated from college, and two of them got a job in California. The other one is working here in Portland. The youngest is a college student and lives in Washington D.C. She is working a summer job there now and has another year before she graduates. I wrote down the name of the college but forgot what it is. [Laughs]
BB: They are Koichi's children, aren't they?
MO: Yes, they are.
BB: How about Noriko?
MO: She has two children, and both of them are married and live in Seattle. They both have an engineering degree, something like mechanical engineering, and have a pretty good job and are doing well. Noriko's elder grandchild has two kids, my great grandchildren. I have two of them. They are four and six, and the older one already stands up to my shoulders.
BB: Where do you live now? Where is your house?
MO: Where do I live? I live in Ladd's Addition. It is close to the church in the southeast area. That's where I live. I have been living in the same house ever since I came over from Japan.
BB: Could you tell us a little bit about the Ikoi No Kai program?
MO: Well, I used to help the program as a volunteer, but I have some health issues and stopped. I try to come over every Tuesday and Thursday and look forward to chatting with all the members.
BB: They serve very good Japanese food too.
MO: I enjoy their Japanese food. We get together, come over and have fun together, and share good news and bad news over a meal. I think it is good for the elderly, and that's why I come here. We can also get the Women's Group updates here. We work on our schedule and decide on locations and activities of our gatherings. I am grateful that I am still able to participate in those various activities I enjoy. [Laughs]
