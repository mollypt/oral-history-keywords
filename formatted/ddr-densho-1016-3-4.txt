AD: I kind of asked this before, but if you were talking to like a junior high student right now, what... just kind of in a nutshell, what would you impart to them about just the nature of freedom and what that means?
PC: Freedom is something that you can't take for granted because it can be taken away so very quickly. It takes one executive order, and then the whole world changes. And it's not something that you can just ignore. It's very easy to take it for granted because we grew up with it, we were all raised with it. And we've had very little to come in to interfere with it. So we can look back on the events of September 11th and other things that have happened since then and say, "We're not as safe as we thought we were," and life is really very short. We don't know when it can end, and it's up to each one of us, no matter how young or old you are, to do whatever part that you have, whether it's behaving responsibly and taking responsibility for your actions. That even young people are part of the world in the community, and they play a very important part in it. 'Cause that's where the hope is for the future, is in the younger people. And if they don't understand what freedom is and how it was achieved and how it was protected or how each cultural group may have defended their own freedom to be here, then they kind of miss out on a lot. 'Cause it isn't just for one group, it's for everybody.
