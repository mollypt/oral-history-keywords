BB: I would like to ask you about your childhood back in Japan if you don't mind. How was your school when you were little?
MO: Let's see. I don't remember well, but it sounds like I had a hard time with Japanese. My grandfather and grandmother sent me to a preschool for two years instead of a grade school. I remember how to fold origami figures because I spent two years learning how to. I was helping other children on my second year there. I was pretty bossy. [Laughs] That's what I did. I didn't have any problems with schools after that. I didn't have any difficulties with Japanese in high school, didn't have any problems after I entered elementary school. I forgot all my English. I was doing fine.
BB: Did you like your schools?
MO: Well, I didn't mind. [Laughs] I wasn't the smartest. I just graduated.
BB: Did you travel on a boat by yourself or with your family when you went to Japan and came back to the States?
MO: My mother took me back first. That was when I was three. I came with my relative when I came back next time. We went directly to Victoria and came down to Seattle. I went back with a direct route when I went back in 1938. We realized that the fare would be the same to come over though Hawaii or though Vancouver when I came back with my family in 1955. We decided to spend some vacation time in Hawaii and went through Hawaii to come back. There was not a direct route from Hawaii to Settle. There was no direct ship route, and we had to go to Vancouver or Victoria. We went to Victoria and spent one extra day there. It would have taken eleven or twelve days from Yokohama directly to Seattle. It took us nineteen days because we went through Hawaii and Canada to come over here. It was a nice trip though. We cannot do it very often. [Laughs] My mother bought us second class tickets. [Laughs] The room was very nice, and there was plenty of space for the children to play around. We had a nice trip. My daughter is still talking about the trip and wants to do it again. She says it would be nicer than the modern plane trip. It was fun. We had a great trip.
BB: What were your grandfather and grandmother like?
MO: I don't remember my grandfather very well. He was very kind and nice, but I don't have a clear memory. My grandmother was a disciplinarian. She was very strict. I remember how strict she was. My aunt was taking care of me. She was my grandparents' son's wife; their in-law was taking care of me. She was their son's wife. My aunt took care of me. I think my grandmother was extra cautious to make sure that I would be good and cause no trouble because my aunt was taking care of me.
BB: You came back to the States in 1937 and got together with your mother again. How did you feel?
MO: [Laughs] Well, foster parents. They say foster parents are closer than biological parents. We felt closer to the foster parents who raised me than to the biological parents who did not live with me. I was feeling closer to my foster parents and wanted to go back to Japan. I wanted to go back to Japan. Ms. Nakata, you know Ms. Ikuko Nakata, she was my classmate in high school and very nice to me when I came over in 1937. We were good friends, and I made more friends. I really missed Japan though. I ended up going back in 1938. [Laughs]
BB: Did you meet your husband before you came to the States?
MO: I rarely saw him. I probably had seen him somewhere, but that's about it. He is one of our relatives, and we had seen him somewhere.
BB: How was your wedding? Did you have your relatives?
MO: It was attended by only our relatives. My parents were here. My uncle and aunt, my husband's parents and siblings, they were there.
BB: Is your husband from the same area? From Okayama prefecture?
MO: Yes, he is from Okayama.
BB: Is there anything else you would like to talk about?
MO: Not really. [Laughs]
BB: It looks like you have a lot of great friends here.
MO: Yes. I do. Everyone is taking good care of me. Everyone is so nice to me, and that makes me realize how old I am. [Laughs] Everyone is so nice, but I don't think I can reciprocate. It is frustrating.
BB: You have a lot of visitors from Japan, don't you?
MO: I do.
BB: You get together.
MO: Yes, we do.
BB: Did you used to volunteer here?
MO: Yes, I did.
BB: When was that?
MO: It has been quite a while. Tomie was still here. Five or six years ago, or longer. I was volunteering for some time when Kazuko, you know Kazuko, was here.
BB: You made a great contribution to the community.
MO: The community is supporting me. Thank you very much.
BB: Thank you very much for your time today.
MO: Thank you very much. [Laughs]
BB: Thank you.
MO: Thank you.
