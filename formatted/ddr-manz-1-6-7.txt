JA: So how did you and your family get acclimated to this kind of life, I mean...
AM: [Laughs] Well, one of the first things we started to do was we tried to water around the apartment so that it wouldn't be so dusty, and then eventually we were able to get some lawn seed and planted a lawn between the barracks, that kind of helped. Now, we lived on the north end of the barracks so we used to get direct wind, the north wind, which was pretty strong. So what we did was we tried to keep the door, which was on the end of the barrack, closed all the time, and then we opened up another side door on the side of the barrack so we won't get the direct wind coming through the door. And then I eventually was able to make a little sink for my mother and let the drain go right into the ground. Of course, she didn't do too much cooking because we all ate at the mess hall. But we tried to plant as much as we can so that it's a little bit more bearable.
JA: And tell me about all the little parks and the ponds and the gardens around.
AM: Oh, you know, there were many landscape specialists in camp. That was their regular businessing while they were, prior to World War II, and there was a man named Mr. Kuichiro Nishi, he was from Venice or West L.A., he had a nursery. And they, you know, they always tried to do something to keep themselves occupied, and also by making a garden and things like that, a Japanese type of a garden, I guess it really took away a lot of the misery, you know, by looking at the beautiful garden. So this man went ahead and made a great big garden, and eventually this garden was called Merritt Park, and it was a beautiful garden. Mr. Ansel Adams took a nice photograph of the garden, and in fact, I used to -- when my wife graduated from high school, I used to take three or four of her friends up to Merritt Park and take pictures for them in their cap and gown at the Merritt Park. People just went over there to really... what is it, something about being in that park made everybody, well, kind of make, forget about the hardship a little bit. That was not the only park that was in camp. Quite a few of the people made their own garden near their mess hall, so you know, when you stand in line for a long time for the mess hall line, you were able to stand next to, watch, and look at the little park that was made. So, it was, well, it's really nice to be in a place like that, make you a little, make you forget a little bit about the hardship.
JA: So people made the desert bloom.
AM: Yes, uh-huh.
JA: Flowers all around the place?
AM: Oh, yes. There was a man who was in the next building to us, he grew a lot of chrysanthemum. Oh it was just beautiful in the fall, you know, when the chrysanthemum flower bloomed. And then there were flower growers from San Fernando who were in Manzanar and they got some seeds and they planted flowers and things like that. So, well, anything to kind of beautify and make it easier. Make life a little easier, you know.
JA: Any roses?
AM: I can't remember any roses, but I remember seeing a lot of chrysanthemum, snapdragon. Oh yeah, there were some roses, come to think of it.
