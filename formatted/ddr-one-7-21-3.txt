BB: Please tell us about your children.
MO: Our first child was born in 1939. She was born two weeks before my husband was deployed to China. He saw the baby and took off. She was four and half years old when he came back. [Laughs] He came back about four years later. That is our eldest daughter. Our second daughter Yoshiko was born in 1945 when everything was tight during the war. That is the year the war ended. She was born in February, and the war ended in August. She just passed away the other day. Our first son was born in 1947. We have three children.
BB: It must have been very rough during the war.
MO: Yes, it was. My father passed away in a camp here in 1943. They sent a notice to us in April through the Red Cross, and we received it in mid-October. They sent a notice to Japan. He died in a camp, and my mother was left alone. My mother came back here right after the war ended and started a tofu business where she used to live.
BB: Did you have any communications with you parents during the war, like letters?
MO: We couldn't communicate with each other at all during the war. My father wrote to me and said it would be the last letter. He wrote he could not offer any support for me and wished me well. That was before the war started. That was it. The offices of the occupation were established in Japan after the war, and a soldier from the General Headquarters came to Okayama to deliver a letter for us. We met the soldier, and we finally found out how my family was doing.
BB: You had no idea how they were doing...
MO: Not at all.
BB: You didn't know what was happening on the other side.
MO: Right.
BB: It must have been hard.
MO: My uncle passed away here right after my father died. We didn't know that until after the war. We just didn't know.
BB: When did your husband come back from China? Was that after the war?
MO: No, he came back before the war ended. He spent almost five years, a bit longer than four years in China. He came back once and worked at a hospital. He got called in again toward the end of the war and was stationed in Yamaguchi prefecture. He didn't experience the air raid. I lived in Okayama prefecture and lived through air raids. He didn't as he was in Yamaguchi. He was still in Yamaguchi when the war ended. When the war ended, it ended on August 15th, he came back and had to walk along the railway in the Hiroshima region. That's how he came back.
