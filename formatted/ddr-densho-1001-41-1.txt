GH: My name is Grant Hirabayashi. I was born in Kent, Washington, which is a suburb of Seattle. Currently, I live in Silver Springs, Maryland.
JD: I'd like to start with a few questions about your early life, adolescence, and growing up. Could you describe the circumstances of your early years? You spent six years going to school in Japan. Could you say something about that?
GH: Actually I spent eight years in Japan. During my early years, I had two friends, Tom Horiuchi and Kenji Nomura, and we're three buddies. Matter of fact, my neighbor called us the three chipmunks. We were the three... same size, same grade, same age. We were fishing one day and the fish wasn't biting and the conversation turns to Japan. Tom and Kenji both had visited Japan during summer vacation. They were talking about their experience in Japan and they were talking about taking their shoes off when they entered the house, they slept on the floor ontatami. Talked about abacus, people driving on the wrong side of the street. That aroused my curiosity. Finally I prevailed upon my father to send me to Japan. But he did say that -- there were eight of us -- and he couldn't afford to send each of us for summer vacation.
[Interruption]
JD: Let's start back just a little bit. Maybe you could tell us what year this was when you and your three friends were talking about Japan and you first got interested. That's wonderful to hear about that. I can just imagine the three of you. And also, when you talk about going to Japan, did you know you were going to be going there for a long time?
[Interruption]
GH: The year we were talking about was 1931. And the year of 1932 I was able to prevail upon my father to send me but with the condition that I would spend two years in Japan to study language. He thought that would be worthwhile. After two years in Japan, I took the examination forchuugakkou, which is little school, and I failed. So after two years, I wanted to come back but I couldn't come back saying that I failed the entrance exam, and I had something to prove. So I stayed one more year and I did pass the examination. After I passed I asked my father to send me a ticket back. He said no. The ticket would be forthcoming when I finishchuugakkou, which is five years. So, I did, I spent a total of eight years in Japan prior to my return.
[Interruption]
GH: I did go over when I was age thirteen. And I came back at age twenty-one.
JD: What happened when you came back to the U.S.? What did you do?
GH: Well, I went back to high school and I went to Kent high school where there was a precedence where they accepted all my credits taken in Japan and thereby enabled me to graduate in one year.
