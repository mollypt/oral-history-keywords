AL: When the, when the park was first established, before the general management plan existed, if you could just step back in your mind and think about what your ultimate vision was, if money were no object, what your vision -- or maybe I shouldn't say if money were no object, but when the site was established, what was your vision for Manzanar, your personal vision?
TO: I wanted something or purpose to tell the story. To tell the story, to educate, to... the story including how in times of crises, that constitutional protections are often ignored, and how it's incumbent on all people to insure the protection of the vulnerable. And so, that was my goal, because I have a civil rights background, I'm a former internee, and I want people to understand that there was a mistake and that we need to learn from this mistake, so that we don't treat groups in times of peril in this way. But it also to me was a message that this is a country that is willing to acknowledge past injustices and educate about that. It took a tremendous amount of Japanese American community advocacy, and that was a very healthy part of our becoming full, 100 percent Americans, and not being apologetic Americans, that that's part of our legacy. Our responsibility, I believe, is having been placed in that position historically as Jews and slaves who become their descendents become the moral conscience for this country. We too have that responsibility, when it comes to treating people or groups by guilt through association, that this country's legal premises revolved around individual commitment of some wrong, and not being treated as a group.
AL: Did that, did that vision translate into a physical picture of what the site would look like? Were you, I mean, did you ever think about guard towers, fences, barracks, any of that stuff?
TO: No, no, I'm really interested in messages. And there are messages about how people thrive and survive and whatever else, and I'm not too much into that. Because it gets away-- that's fine to show the resilience of people and whatever else, and all, I think that's great. But the first message is the deprivation, the wrongful, unjust deprivation without any due process. And how we as a people, through our efforts, won back our respect and the national apology and some compensation. But that to me is the important message, how it's depicted isn't so important, so I was part of drafting that legislation. I approached the authors and I have cut a lot of the deals in terms of telling all the stories and stuff, and limiting the scope of construction, and all.
