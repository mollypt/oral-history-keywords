AL: And when you were discharged, where did you go?
YK: I came back with a group to Seattle, Washington, came down to Sacramento, was discharged from Sacramento, came to L.A. where the families came and met us.
AL: How did you start over?
YK: Well, I lived with my wife's family, and that time, they were giving GI Bill of Rights. So picked up teaching because that's what I was doing in Japan, teaching English to the workers.
AL: So where did you go to school?
YK: Went to USC because in Japan, you had to teach them something, so have them do what was done, needed. So I found that, well, since I could speak a little Japanese, it's easier for my [inaudible] to do something like that.
AL: Was it difficult to get a teaching job after the war?
YK: After the war, it was not too difficult, because there weren't many Japanese American teachers. I knew of only one who had gone through UCLA. But after our group of GIs became teachers, we got a whole, everyone wanted to teach.
AL: Where did you go teach?
YK: The only place open, and this is true of big districts in big cities, well, we had a choice of inner city. And so we ended up in the south central part of Los Angeles.
AL: And was Masa a teacher at that time, also?
YK: No, she was raising the family.
