[This transcript is a translation of the original Japanese text.]
BB: How did you feel about going back to Japan after the war?
MO: I guess I am optimistic to a fault. [Laughs] When I came over for the first time, I knew I could always go back to Japan if it didn't work out for me. I was fully intended to settle down here when I came back with my family for the second time. I was not too anxious. Whatever happens will happen.
BB: Where did you live? Did you live in the tofu store? Or somewhere else?
MO: We were staying at a hotel for a while right after we arrived. Then we bought a house, my mother bought a house for us. We moved into the house in 1957, and I have been living in the same house since 1957.
BB: How were your children?
MO: Well, as for the children, the eldest was fifteen when we came over, and she was enrolled in the eighth grade in grade school. She had graduated from a junior high school in Japan. She entered into the eighth grade and spent one year, but she did not speak English. She had English classes in Japan and could recognize the alphabet. She learned a bit in Japan. So we put her in school with white students. The teachers there were concerned about her because they didn't know if she was ready for high school after spending only one year at school here. They took very good care of her and took her to school and made sure that she would get to school on the bus and into the school building. She stayed at the high school and graduated from Lincoln High School in four years. She was a bit older, and I recommended she take business classes. She became fluent in both Japanese and English. She might not be an excellent writer or reader, but both her English and Japanese are sufficient. She can do it all in both languages. That's why she got a job at an airport and worked there for a long time with her language skills. The second child was in the fifth grade in Japan and enrolled in the fifth grade here. She was in the fifth grade. She was a strong student and allowed to skip the sixth grade and enroll in the seventh grade. She spent one year, skipped to the seventh grade, graduated from high school and graduated from Oregon State University. She got a job with Pan Am and flew all over the world on a plane. [Laughs] That's what they did. She took me and my husband to a trip to Japan, Hong Kong, Thailand and Europe in 1970.
BB: How did you like it?
MO: It was great. [Laughs] It was over thirty years ago, but I never forget. I still remember. Ko (Koichi) just went to school, graduated from college and that's all. [Laughs]
BB: The tofu store is still contributing to the community.
MO: I cannot do anything. I'm trying. My husband ran the business alone in his days, and my mother worked hard all by herself too. The store has twelve or thirteen employees now and the entire family is engaged. They are doing well and keeping busy. I am grateful.
