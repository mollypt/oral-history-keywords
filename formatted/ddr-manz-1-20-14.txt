AL: So what is your recollection of -- well, before we leave Rohwer, do you have any other memories of the camp that you, come to mind?
TO: I'd like to talk about a Christmas party. I remember a Christmas party, big old jolly Santa Claus, and in the mess hall, they would have programs, and when you get in line, sit on Santa's lap, get a gift. You had to remember, around Rohwer, it was, there's just very poor communities. That area was very rural and... but we all managed to get a gift. And I got this funny little trinket, made out of I don't even know whether they had plastic then, some kind of little pressed bracelet and necklace. And probably didn't cost a nickel, but I just thought it was just wonderful. It was nice that the townspeople did something, whether it was their churches, to do something for those children in the camp.
AL: So these presents came from the local community? And was the Santa Claus from the... was he Anglo, or Japanese American?
TO: Yeah.
AL: Interesting. Any other holidays, that you remember there?
TO: I remember that in the mess hall they had different programs, I know, women would perform, dancing. Because how I know that is, we would, we would practice and mimic them when we played, after we would do all this Japanese head turning and all. [Laughs] And one time we had a Hawaiian act, and my mom and dad tells me that when they weren't looking, I ran up on the stage, and there I was, "Manuela boy, my dear boy," and my mother said, "Go get her, go get her." And I just finished the dance and did my bow, and came and sat back down. That's a happy memory, and how -- I don't whether I remember it, but the family keeps telling the story, right? Telling the story.
