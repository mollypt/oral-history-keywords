AD: Talk about how life changed for you. You had been interned, then you were in jail, and when you came out, when did your life become normal again? You got married, tell us a little bit about how it became, how you regained your freedom and your life.
GA: Within a few months after I came out, I made friends with a lady that, that was I guess you would refer to as "love at first sight." She had no objection as to what I did, my background, she said, "You did what you thought was right." And so we were, we started to go around together, and two years later, we got married." And I raised a family of three. And during that time when I was raising the family, why, I kept a low profile, not knowing what the community thought of me. So I was pretty much to myself and my family and the relatives. And when my oldest son was about junior high, going into high school, which is about, what, twelve, thirteen years old, I thought I'd bring my kids together and tell them before they may hear words from others, kids who had their parents go to service, say, "Hey, your dad was just a draft dodger." Rather than to have that happen, I thought I'd just gather them around my, the kitchen table, we'll talk about that. Because they said that they did read -- my oldest son, anyway -- read about the evacuation, the incarceration of the Japanese, and it only consisted of about one little paragraph and nothing else. And so I decided to explain to them what had happened and why I did what I did, and also that I have an amnesty from the president for my step. And they, they realized that and they said, "Well, gee, I don't blame you, Dad." So anyway, as it turned out, none of the kids had come up with a remark like that, that, "Your dad was a draft dodger," so the life was pretty good.
And I was starting to get out more, get more comfortable, and my wife being, began to work, wound up working for Frederick & Nelson, they're no longer there, but while there she found a friend. And this lady that I had started to go around with, she was working there, too, so they got to know each other. And the invitation to their house, the, her husband was a vet who was shot up so badly that they almost gave him up for dead. But they invited us time and again, and I thought, "Well, gee, maybe he doesn't know what I did." But after two or three offers, I said, "Well, let's go out there and see what he has to say." And so we went, we were invited to dinner, and as it turned out, he was a heck of a nice fellow. And we got to know him real well, and I liked him very much. Unfortunately, I didn't know, didn't know him long enough to really get acquainted with him. But everything in a nutshell, that's what it was. Gradually I opened, well, searched out, reached out further and further and found out that people are understanding. And, well, this is as of lately, but there's a lot of people that are very interested about the resisters, only they call us 'no-no boys,' which is a bad term they use because as I told you, I was not a 'no-no boy.' I refused to go, but I also said I would serve if we are attacked, my own country is attacked. So I am not a 'no-no boy,' but they all grouped us together. And so still, people call us the 'no-no boys,' and I try to correct them. And this is one of the reasons why I want to correct everybody, that we are not, a lot of us, are not a person that would turn against their own country. We had certain things that we wanted to prove, and as it turned out, we came from all different camps, but we all had the same idea in our mind, but the people wouldn't, wouldn't respect us for it.

GA: It's a different story now, yeah. The truth is finally coming out, and as I showed you, the plaque, tray they gave me, this is given by the JACL, Japanese Citizens League, and... GA: I finished talking to the middle school the other day, and as my last statement, what I told them was that, "Study hard, finish high school, go to college. That they are the future citizens of our country, that they're are gonna be, they are gonna be leading the country someday.
