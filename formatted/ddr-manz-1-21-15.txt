AL: I know you're asked about this all of the time, but the, the Olivers Club, could you describe what the Olivers were and how you were involved, and what you did?
YK: Well, to begin with, going back to where we lived, on Central Avenue, Central Avenue was a natural border. Alameda was a no-man's-land, had railroad tracks, so Central Avenue was the beginning of our gang, Central, Central Avenue gang. The other street was Turner Street that went where Fukui Mortuary is, that was the Turner Street gang. That's what, when we were pre-teens. So even though we went to the same public school and the same Japanese school, there was no, really no hard feelings because we were still in the same gang.
AL: So you weren't, there wasn't rivalry.
YK: Well, not really.
AL: No drive-by shootings, except with the BB gun? [Laughs]
YK: No, no, that didn't come.
AL: That was just the Russians, okay. [Laughs]
YK: So because of little rivalry, and we were really into the Japanese movies, the samurai sword-fighting, so we would have swordfights, that Central Avenue gang and the Turner Street gang. We'd make our own wooden swords.
AL: Are these both Japanese American gangs?
YK: Yeah. They would have, make swords out of wood, and we went, we had couple of movie stars who were our idols.
AL: Who were they?
YK: Miyamoto Musashi, and he was our... well, both sides had him as an idol. So we would meet behind Union Church, big open space, honor system swordfight. So we had fights there, and we would have, every year, a swordfight. Then that turned into kick-the-can games.
AL: How do you play kick-the-can?
YK: Well, you have a lot of people on... well, this is one group. You have one person who is "it," we have a can in the middle of our yard, and someone who was picked to be "it" has to retrieve the can after it's kicked by, well, one team. Well, you kick the can and everyone hides. So the "it" has to get the can, put it in the middle of the yard, and he has to go look for the, whoever is hiding. And when he finds the guy, he calls the name and, calls the name and tags the can and then they take turns hiding, catching more people. And so it went all day, kicking the can, hiding. So that was one of our game.
AL: So how did the Olivers change that?
YK: Well, our gangs got together, and from there, games turned into a softball game. And in order to have enough people for a softball team, you had to combine some of the better players. So by process of elimination, some people didn't like softball, some people liked football. So we, when the season came, we played football, and the older boys would become our coaches. And because we had quite a few players to pick from, it became... well, it was a necessity to pick out the better players. So we had, oh, older team and a younger team. The Oliver became one when they got teams, started practicing together, and then we met Ms. Oliver, she asked us to come to a meeting.
AL: Who was she?
YK: Ms. Oliver, Ms. Nellie G. Oliver, who was a former schoolteacher from the neighborhood. She taught primary grades and was involved in the community.
AL: Did she teach at Amelia Street?
YK: Yeah, she did at one, she taught at another school south of First Street, which was called Hewitt Street, which was closer to the Maryknoll Church.
