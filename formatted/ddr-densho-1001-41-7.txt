JD: When did you, when you were in the military, when did you hear about the bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki?
GH: Well, talking about the atom bomb, I was in Chungking. I was in charge of the Japanese POW, Air Corps POW. And one day and officer was brought in and I interrogated him. During the course of interrogation he pushed a Chinese matchbox in front of me and he said they were developing a bomb this size that could destroy a city. I said, "You've got to be kidding." Well he re-composed himself and he said, this study, research was being done at Tokyo University, and Kyoto Imperial University, and Osaka University. He did mention that the study at Tokyo Imperial University was under Doctor Hiroshi Nishina, a physicist, a well-known physicist, and sponsored by the army. Whereas the study at Kyoto Imperial University was sponsored by the navy. He then on went on to talk about the bomb and was so technical it was beyond my comprehension, talking about the Cyclotone, fusion, Uranium 235. That was beyond my comprehension. When I returned to Chungking, I would spend one week at the POW camp and then I would come back and spend one week to write up the report. When I came back, there were two officers who had just graduated from the officer's... had just graduated OCS, Officers Candidate School. When I was introduced I did mention about the interview I had with this scientist. And they both rolled their eyes as if to say I had been overseas too long. They assured me that they had graduated from the OCS and they had the latest information and that there was no animal called atomic bomb. The following Monday I started writing, but I felt that I had to write something about the technical aspect of the bomb to make the report worthy. So, not being able to decipher my own writing of interrogation, I said I need help. I did make an appointment with Lieutenant Colonel John Burden who was the head of SINTIC, Sino Intelligence Translation and Interrogation Center. He did give me an appointment and I reported, but there was no response. I think it was dismissed.
When the bomb did fall, I immediately got a jeep and I went to the POW camp and I asked for the POW and I did inform him that we had just dropped a bomb on Hiroshima. I recalled he covered his face and says, "The war must be over." I said, "It's not over, but I think it won't be too long." That was my experience.
JD: That's very interesting. So most people don't know about Japan's attempt to develop an atom bomb. It sounds like you actually knew about the Japanese work on that before most people even in the U.S. military knew about this.
GH: Right.
