AL: How did you leave the Philippines, or why?
YK: Because America had signed the peace treaty, and they had signed on the shipMissouri, and the McArthur was already on the way to Japan. We were attached to his GHQ, so they said, "Well, we're going to Japan now, so pack up."
AL: What is GHQ?
YK: General Headquarters.
AL: So did you work directly with McArthur, I mean, did you --
YK: No, we were attached to his headquarters. When we got to Japan, most of the [inaudible] were stationed in another part of Tokyo. All the translators, interpreters were there. We were detached with another group, small group of translators, and so we were with McArthur's headquarters. He had his headquarters at this largest Japanese insurance company, we were next building. So we did work for acquiring more office spaces for the headquarters, we're to send more interpreters, we're to send more translators. So, because there was a headquarters for General McArthur. We were right in town across the emperor's palace.
AL: Were you there when the emperor came to visit McArthur?
YK: We were on the side watching.
AL: What was that like?
YK: Well, the Japanese people revere the emperor as a god, so they would have their heads bowed onto the ground, as were his, as were his advisors. And so it was a, I don't know, I think it was heartbreak for the Japanese people, but then on the other hand, it turned around, because McArthur had preached democracy, and that became the byword in Japan.
AL: Did you have much interaction with the Japanese people?
YK: No, not on the lower level, because we dealt with the custodians and caretakers of buildings, hiring of cooks for the... but of course, there were others who did the trial interpretation.
